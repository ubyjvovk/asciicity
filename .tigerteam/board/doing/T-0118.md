## Goal

T-0112 found (and flagged, per its ticket) that deck humps match by road
NAME, so Sydney's `Cahill Walk` — whose name covers BOTH the Harbour
Bridge east walkway (1503 m chain) and the elevated Circular Quay walkway
(661 m chain) — gets the 49 m-ASL bridge apex applied to the Circular
Quay chain too: that walkway now floats ~45 m over the quay, right where
the default spawn stands. Rule change: a hump entry applies, per matching
name, only to that name's LONGEST chain; other chains keep the plain
abutment lerp.

## Context

- The hump→chain association happens in two places, both via
  `humpApex(roadName, humps)` after `chainBridgeRoads`:
  `BridgeDecks` in `src/world/terrain.ts` (constructor, ~line 336) and
  the ribbon builder in `src/world/roads.ts` (its own `humpApex` copy,
  ~line 46). Add ONE exported helper in `terrain.ts` — given the full
  chain list, the chain, and the humps, return the apex only when the
  chain is the longest (by polyline arc length; ties: first wins) among
  chains matching that name — and use it from both files (drop the
  roads.ts copy).
- Chain lengths for the motivating case are in T-0112's report
  (`.tigerteam/board/done/T-0112.md`, "Cahill Walk chain finding").
- The rule is a no-op for every existing bridge: GGB/NYC sidewalk and
  roadway names chain into a single chain each (their tests must pass
  unchanged), and Sydney's `Bradfield Highway` / `Harbour Bridge
  Cycleway` keep their aprons because their longest chains ARE the deck.
- `docs/architecture.md`: amend the §4.9 deck-humps wording (and the
  §4.16b as-built note that flagged this) with the longest-chain rule —
  one or two sentences each.
