a building relation whose outer boundary is **16 separate ways**, and the
converter only emits building relations whose outer members are single
closed ways (T-0110 report: one of 24 skipped relations). Teach the
building path to stitch multi-way outer members into closed rings — the
water/woods paths already do exactly this — then refetch Sydney so the
Opera House (and the other skipped relations) land.

**1. Confirmed: the fix lives in the WATER path** of

fixtures. New unit fixtures live in `tests/osm-convert.test.ts` (a
water-relations describe block).

**2. Dedup design — your option A with these locked parameters:**
- Applies to assembled OUTER rings with |area| ≥ 0.1 km² only (small
  ponds skip the O(n²) work).
- Sort those rings by |area| descending; walk down; for each candidate
  compute `coverage` = the fraction of 50 m-grid cells over the
  candidate's bbox whose centres are inside the candidate AND inside
  any already-KEPT ring. Drop the candidate iff `coverage ≥ 0.85`;
  otherwise keep. Deterministic, no polygon booleans.
- Expected outcome on Sydney: Port Jackson kept, Sydney Harbour dropped
  (≈ full coverage); Parramatta River / Lane Cove River kept (adjacent
  bodies, low coverage). If the six parity points still fail after this
  (e.g. two KEPT rings overlap more than expected), block again with
  the coverage numbers — do not tune the 0.85/50 m constants.

**3. Inner holes are now in scope — with this shape:**
- Emit inner-member rings ONLY from relations at least one of whose
  outer rings was KEPT (a dropped duplicate relation contributes no
  islands — that kills most cross-relation island duplicates for free).
- Assemble inners with the same stitcher; an open inner chain is
  skipped and counted, never emitted partially.
- Residual island dedup: skip an inner ring whose centroid lies inside
  an already-emitted island ring.
- Islands are emitted as bare water rings (no names, no landmarks) and
  ride the existing wave-8/9 odd-parity conventions in collision and
  dem flattening unchanged.
